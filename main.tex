\begin{abstract}
This paper documents the firm-size wage premium in Colombia using household survey data for 2008--2025. The sample includes employees, own-account workers, employers, and other workers with positive main-job labor income. We regress log real hourly labor income on firm-size indicators, progressively adding controls and allowing size profiles to differ by status in employment and formality. The most flexible specification controls for worker characteristics and sector-department-year fixed effects. Relative to solo workers of the same employment and formality group, the premium in firms with over 100 workers is about 16 percent for both formal and informal private-sector employees. Among own-account workers, it is 54 percent for formal and 46 percent for informal workers. The estimated changes between 2008 and 2025 do not establish a common widening of the informal premium. We then use these estimates to construct illustrative worker reallocation scenarios. Assigning all workers to formal employment in firms with over 100 workers, while retaining their status in employment, raises predicted average weekly labor income by 44.9 percent, the largest increase among the four scenarios. Requiring at least 30 pooled observations in each destination cell reduces that increase to 26.1 percent. Even assuming that output per hour rose proportionately with predicted labor income, most of Colombia's productivity gap with the United States would remain.
\end{abstract}
