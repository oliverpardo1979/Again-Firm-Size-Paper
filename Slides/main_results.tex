\begin{frame}{The Preferred Specification}
  \small
  \begin{align*}
  \ln w_{iosdt}={}&\alpha+\phi F_i+
    \sum_{g\ne g_0}(\beta_g+\theta_g F_i)D_{ig}\\
    &+X_i'\gamma+\rho_o+\lambda_{s\times d\times t}+\varepsilon_{iosdt}.
  \end{align*}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $F_i$ indicates pension contribution. $D_{ig}$ indicates firm-size category.
    \item $X_i$ includes sex, age, age squared, and education.
    \item $\rho_o$ controls for occupational position, including own-account work.
    \item $\lambda_{s\times d\times t}$ compares workers within the same sector, department, and year.
    \item Solo work is the reference within each formality group: $\beta_{g_0}=\theta_{g_0}=0$.
  \end{itemize}
  %\source{Paper Equation~(\paperref{eq:main}) and Table~\paperref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness}, column (4). 5,268,375 observations. Baseline inference uses 559 sector-department clusters.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interpretation and Policy}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Persistent gaps for comparable workers motivate attention to barriers to formal employment and firm growth.
    %\item Formal 101+ workers also earn more than intermediate-size formal workers. Growth within formal employment should not be ignored.
    \item Support for informal firms should not subsidize remaining informal or weaken incentives to comply.
    \item The premium can also reflect unobserved worker characteristics and employer pay practices. Neither the regressions nor the income scenarios identify productivity gains.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item The preferred 101+/solo premium is 37.1\% among informal workers and 16.5\% among formal workers. Formal workers earn more within every size category.
    \item The informal size-wage relationship is steeper. %Approximate elasticities are 0.097 for informal and 0.040 for formal workers.
    \item The formal 101+/solo estimate in 2025 is close to its 2008 level. The informal estimate rises substantially.
    \item Allocating everyone to formal 101+ jobs implies about 25\% higher total predicted labor income. This is not a causal policy effect.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{frame}
