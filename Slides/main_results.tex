\begin{frame}{The Preferred Specification}
  \small
  \begin{align*}
  \ln w_{iosdt}={}&\alpha+\phi F_i+
    \sum_{g\ne g_0}(\beta_g+\theta_g F_i)D_{ig}\\
    &+X_i'\gamma+\rho_o+\lambda_{s\times d\times t}+\varepsilon_{iosdt}.
  \end{align*}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $F_i$ indicates pension contribution. $D_{ig}$ indicates firm-size category.
    \item $X_i$ includes sex, age, age squared, and education.
    \item $\rho_o$ controls for status in employment, including own-account work.
    \item $\lambda_{s\times d\times t}$ compares workers within the same sector, department, and year.
    \item Solo work is the reference within each formality group: $\beta_{g_0}=\theta_{g_0}=0$.
  \end{itemize}
  %\source{Paper Equation~(\paperref{eq:main}) and Table~\paperref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness}, column (4). 5,426,174 observations. Baseline inference uses 559 sector-department clusters.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interpretation and Policy}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Persistent conditional gaps motivate attention to barriers to formal employment and firm growth. They do not identify the effects of removing those barriers.
    %\item Formal 101+ workers also earn more than intermediate-size formal workers. Growth within formal employment should not be ignored.
    \item Support for informal firms should not subsidize remaining informal or weaken incentives to comply.
    \item If output per hour rose proportionately with predicted labor income, the joint scenario would raise Colombia's productivity from about 22\% to 28\% of the US level, ceteris paribus.
  \end{itemize}
  \vspace{0.25cm}
  \idea{Nearly all of the international productivity gap would remain, even under this comprehensive reassignment.}
  \note{OECD Compendium of Productivity Indicators 2026, Figure 2.1. GDP per hour worked in 2024, purchasing power parity. The comparison assumes proportional income and productivity changes. It does not measure a causal productivity gain.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Firm size is associated with income beyond the formal-informal divide: the full-sample 101+/solo premiums are 37.0\% for informal and 17.2\% for formal workers.
    \item Among salaried workers, both premiums are about 21\%. The larger informal premium in the full sample does not describe employees alone.
    \item The informal 101+/solo estimate rises between 2008 and 2025 in both samples. The statistical evidence of an increase is stronger in the full sample and is not conclusive among salaried workers.
    \item The scenarios imply substantial income gains for reassigned workers. Even if productivity rose proportionately with income, nearly all of the gap with the US would remain.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
