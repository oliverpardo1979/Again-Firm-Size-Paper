\title{Beyond Formality: The Firm-Size Wage Premium in Colombia}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This paper documents the firm-size wage premium in Colombia using household survey data for 2008--2025. The full sample includes salaried workers, own-account workers, employers, and other workers with positive main-job labor income. We regress log real hourly labor income on firm-size indicators interacted with formality, controlling for worker characteristics, status in employment, and sector-department-year fixed effects. We repeat the analysis for a salaried workers sample. Relative to solo workers of the same formality status, the premium in firms with over 100 workers is 37 percent for informal and 17 percent for formal workers in the full sample. In the salaried workers sample, it is about 21 percent for both formal and informal workers. We then use these estimates to construct illustrative worker reallocation scenarios. The largest increase in predicted average weekly labor income comes from assigning all workers in each sample to formal employment in firms with over 100 workers. Predicted average weekly labor income across all workers in each sample rises by at most 25.9 percent in the full sample and 12.3 percent in the salaried workers sample. Even assuming that output per hour rose proportionately with predicted labor income, nearly all of Colombia's productivity gap with the United States would remain.
\end{abstract}
