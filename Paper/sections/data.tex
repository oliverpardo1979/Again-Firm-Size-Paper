\section{Data}

The empirical analysis uses microdata from the Gran Encuesta Integrada de Hogares (GEIH), Colombia's nationally representative labor force survey produced by the National Administrative Department of Statistics (DANE). Our sample covers 2008--2019 and 2021--2025. We exclude 2020 because of pandemic-related data limitations \citep{DANE2020GEIH}. The data combine separate annual samples rather than following the same workers over time.

The sample is restricted to employed workers with positive labor income, valid weekly hours, and positive survey expansion weights. An expansion weight indicates how many workers in the surveyed population a respondent represents. We use these weights to calculate employment shares and income averages. We convert reported monthly labor income and weekly hours to a common hourly basis using 12 months and 52 weeks per year. Let \(i\) index workers and \(t\) survey years. Denote reported monthly labor income by \(MonthlyIncome_{it}\), weekly hours by \(WeeklyHours_{it}\), and the end-of-year Consumer Price Index by \(CPI_t\). Real hourly labor income in constant 2025 Colombian pesos, \(w_{it}\), is

\begin{equation}
  w_{it} =
  \frac{12 \times MonthlyIncome_{it}}{52 \times WeeklyHours_{it}}\times
  \frac{CPI_{2025}}{CPI_t}.    
\end{equation}
The factors 12 and 52 convert the reported monthly income and weekly hours to the same time unit. They are not observations of each worker's annual income or hours. When reporting implied monthly income, we reverse the hourly conversion by multiplying real hourly income by reported weekly hours and \(52/12\).

Firm size is measured using the worker-reported firm-size variable in GEIH. We harmonize the categories into the following ordered groups: Solo, 2--3, 4--5, 6--10, 11--19, 20--30, 31--50, 51--100, and 101+. Solo identifies respondents who report working alone. It is distinct from occupational position, which identifies whether they are own-account workers, employees, or employers. The newer questionnaire separates 101--200 and 201+ workers. We combine both categories into 101+ to maintain comparability with earlier years. The data do not contain employer identifiers, so we cannot link respondents who work for the same firm.

The harmonized education variable distinguishes no education, preschool, primary, lower secondary, upper secondary, and higher education, as well as a separate ``does not know/not reported'' response. The descriptive education comparisons combine no education and preschool into ``preschool or less,'' and the two secondary categories into ``secondary.'' Only these descriptive education comparisons omit the ``does not know/not reported'' group.

Formality is defined using pension contribution status. Workers who contribute to pensions are classified as formal, while workers who do not contribute are classified as informal. This definition follows the social-protection approach used in Latin American labor-market measurement, which identifies informality through lack of access to social-security benefits such as pension or health insurance \citep{WorldBankLACInformality2026}. This is a worker-level measure of formality, not a measure of the firm's legal registration.

Economic activity is harmonized across revisions of Colombia's industrial classification (CIIU) into broad sectors. The geographic variable identifies departments, treating Bogot\'a as a department-equivalent Capital District.
Occupational position distinguishes private-sector employees, government
employees, domestic workers, own-account workers, employers, unpaid family
workers, unpaid workers in other households, day laborers, and other workers.
We harmonize the occupational codes by survey framework using the corresponding questionnaire categories \citep{DANE2025GEIHQuestionnaire}.

The initial sample contains 5,353,440 worker observations pooled across survey years. Of these, 5,268,514 have complete information on the variables just described, together with sex and age. A recorded ``does not know/not reported'' education response is retained as its own category and is distinct from a missing value.
