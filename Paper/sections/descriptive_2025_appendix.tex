Table~\ref{tab:minimum-wage-bunching-2025} complements Figure~\ref{fig:minwage-distribution-formality-size} by quantifying the share of workers below, near, and above the hourly minimum-wage benchmark in 2025. Table~\ref{tab:minimum-wage-monthly-bunching-2025} reports the analogous exercise using monthly labor income relative to the monthly minimum wage. The alternative-reference tables use workers in firms with 101 or more workers or with 11--19 workers as the reference category. In the trend tables, the sign indicates whether the relative wage ratio rises or falls over time. The interaction-coefficient tables separately report informal size gaps, the formal-minus-informal difference in those gaps, and formal size gaps. Their middle column is not itself a wage gap relative to the reference size.

Tables~\ref{tab:status-cells}--\ref{tab:status-clustering} document the
cell sizes and complete profiles behind Section~\ref{subsec:status-results}.
The observed status-size-formality cells cover 127 of the 162 possible
combinations. Empty cells are not assigned coefficients. The reporting
thresholds flag small comparisons rather than establish sufficient overlap
between workers. In particular, government and employer solo records require
year-specific checking against questionnaire instructions. They remain in
the estimation sample but do not serve as published reference categories.

Table~\ref{tab:status-income-contributions} separates the joint scenario by
the worker's original status in employment. Own-account workers and employers
contribute 37.8 percentage points of the 44.9 percent total increase.
These contributions use baseline predicted income shares, not employment
shares.
\input{Paper/sections/status_income_contributions_table}

Table~\ref{tab:income-counterfactual-support} restricts moves according to
the number of observations in the destination cell, defined by sector,
department, status in employment, size, and formality. Restricted workers
stay at origin and remain in the denominator. In the informal-to-informal
101+ scenario, requiring 30 observations in 2025 permits no moves.
There are 1,843 informal 101+ observations that year, but no such
destination cell contains more than 25. This is not a zero estimate of
the return to an informal move. It is a scenario in which the stated
destination rule permits none. These checks assess extrapolation, not
job-transition feasibility.
