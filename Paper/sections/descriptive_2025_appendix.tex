\section{Additional Tables}

This appendix reports the minimum-wage distribution table, alternative-reference tables, and robustness checks referenced in Sections~4 and 5. Table~\ref{tab:minimum-wage-bunching-2025} complements Figure~\ref{fig:minwage-distribution-formality-size} by quantifying the share of workers below, near, and above the statutory hourly minimum wage in 2025. The first set of alternative-reference tables uses workers in firms with 101 or more workers or with 11--19 workers as the reference category. These changes alter the normalization but do not change the underlying empirical specifications. In each table, the sign of a coefficient indicates whether the listed category is above or below the omitted category under the same controls.

Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness} adds department-level fixed effects. In Colombia, departments are first-level territorial units, with Bogot\'a treated as a department-equivalent Capital District. The most demanding geography specification absorbs sector-department-year fixed effects, so the firm-size coefficients are identified within the same sector, department, and year. The final column adds occupational-position controls to this specification. The large-firm coefficient remains positive and statistically significant: workers in firms with 101 or more employees earn about 40.2 percent more than comparable solo workers after conditioning on sector-department-year cells and occupational position. Tables~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness-101-reference} and \ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness-11-19-reference} report the same robustness checks with alternative reference categories. Tables~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness}--\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness-11-19-reference} repeat the local-geography robustness exercise allowing the firm-size coefficients to differ between formal and informal workers. The coefficients in those tables are within-formality comparisons, so each panel compares workers with the same formality status.
