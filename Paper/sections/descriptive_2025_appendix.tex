The first set of tables uses workers in firms with 101 or more workers as the reference category. This change reverses the comparison but does not change the underlying empirical specifications. Negative coefficients indicate that the listed category earns less than the 101+ category under the same controls.

Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness} adds department-level fixed effects. In Colombia, departments are first-level territorial units, with Bogot\'a treated as a department-equivalent Capital District. The most demanding geography specification absorbs sector-department-year fixed effects, so the firm-size coefficients are identified within the same sector, department, and year. The final column adds occupational-position controls to this specification. The large-firm coefficient remains positive and statistically significant: workers in firms with 101 or more employees earn about 40.2 percent more than comparable solo workers after conditioning on sector-department-year cells and occupational position. Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness-101-reference} reports the same robustness checks with 101+ workers as the reference category.
