\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{From Simple to Preferred Specifications}
\label{subsec:preferred-specification}

We begin with a regression of log real hourly labor income on firm-size
indicators alone. We then add sector-year fixed effects, worker
characteristics, and formality in successive columns of
Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-wage-premium-regressions}. The final column is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-premium}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0} b_g D_{ig}
  + X_{ist}'\gamma + \phi F_{ist}
  + \lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist}.
\end{equation}
Here, \(i\), \(s\), and \(t\) index workers, sectors, and survey years.
The indicator \(D_{ig}\) identifies firm-size category \(g\), with solo
work as the reference \(g_0\). The vector \(X\) includes a female-worker
indicator, age, age squared, and education dummies, and \(F\) indicates pension contribution. All four
columns use the same 5,268,514 observations and GEIH expansion weights.
This sequence describes how the size coefficients change after accounting
for observed differences between workers and sectors. It does not decompose
the wage gap into causal contributions. Even after adding \(F\), the model
constrains each size coefficient \(b_g\) to be the same for formal and
informal workers.

Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness} retains the common-premium
restriction and adds geographic controls and occupational position.
Departments are Colombia's first-level territorial units, with Bogot\'a
treated as a department-equivalent Capital District. Column (1) repeats
Equation~(\ref{eq:common-premium}). Column (2) adds department-year fixed
effects. Column (3) instead includes one fixed effect for each combination
of sector, department, and year. Column (4) also controls for occupational
position.

The last two columns exclude 139 observations in cells containing only one
observation, leaving 5,268,375 observations. Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-wage-premium-regressions}
clusters standard errors by sector. Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-robustness}
uses sector-department clustering, as does the preferred model below.
Changing clustering affects inference, not coefficient estimates.

Finally, our preferred specification allows the firm-size wage premium to differ between
formal and informal workers. It also accounts for occupational position and
compares workers within the same economic sector, department, and year:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
  \ln(w_{isdt}) = \alpha + \phi F_{isdt}
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0}(\beta_g+\theta_g F_{isdt})D_{ig}
  + X_{isdt}'\gamma + \rho_{o(i)} + \lambda_{s\times d\times t}
  + \varepsilon_{isdt}.
\end{equation}
Here, \(d\) indexes departments, and the outcome, size indicators, formality
indicator, and worker controls retain their definitions above. Occupational-position fixed
effects \(\rho_{o(i)}\), where \(o(i)\) denotes worker \(i\)'s occupational
position, distinguish employment relationships, including
own-account work, employer status, and private- and public-sector wage
employment.

The sector-department-year fixed effects absorb wage differences common to
workers in the same sector and department in a given year. The size
coefficients compare firm-size categories within these cells, separately by
formality and conditional on the included worker and occupational-position
controls. This is regression adjustment, not exact matching of workers on
every characteristic. Controlling for occupational position
is relevant because solo work and employment in larger firms differ substantially
in the prevalence of own-account and wage employment. These controls describe
conditional comparisons, rather than identifying the total effect of moving
from own-account work to a salaried job.

We estimate the model with GEIH expansion weights and cluster standard errors
by sector-department cells. Column (4) of
Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness} reports this
preferred specification. Columns (1)--(3) provide comparisons with progressively
more detailed geographic controls. The estimation sample for column (4)
contains 5,268,375 observations.

Within informal employment, the log-wage gap relative to solo work is
\(\beta_g\). Within formal employment, it is \(\beta_g+\theta_g\). For any
estimated log-wage contrast \(\widehat{\Delta}\), we report the percentage gap
as \(100[\exp(\widehat{\Delta})-1]\). These are ratios of exponentiated
conditional mean log incomes, not automatically ratios of arithmetic mean
incomes. Translating fitted log incomes into levels for the income scenarios
requires the additional retransformation assumption described in
Section~\ref{subsec:income-counterfactuals}. Statistical tests use the variance of the
full linear contrast, including the covariances between its coefficients.
For all regression specifications, two-sided tests and 95 percent confidence
intervals use a Student t distribution with \(G-1\) degrees of freedom, where
\(G\) is the number of clusters in the estimation sample. This gives 16 degrees
of freedom in the sector-clustered comparison models and 558 in the preferred
sector-department-clustered model. The same rule applies to coefficients and their linear
combinations, regardless of the reference category.

The model also distinguishes three comparisons. For an origin size \(g\) and a
destination size \(h\), which may be larger or smaller, they are:
\begin{align}
  \Delta^{F}_{g\to h} &= (\beta_h+\theta_h)-(\beta_g+\theta_g), \nonumber\\
  \Delta^{I}_{g\to h} &= \beta_h-\beta_g, \label{eq:preferred-contrasts}\\
  \Delta^{I\to F}_{g} &= \phi+\theta_g. \nonumber
\end{align}
The first two compare firm sizes within formal and informal employment,
respectively. The third compares formal with informal employment at the same
firm size, with informal workers as the denominator. We set
\(\beta_{g_0}=\theta_{g_0}=0\). Changing the omitted size category to 101+ or
11--19 workers leaves fitted log incomes unchanged. A given pairwise contrast
and its standard error are also unchanged, although the coefficients displayed
relative to the new base differ. These are adjusted wage comparisons. Interpreting them as the gains
from actual worker movements requires additional assumptions about selection
and the response of employers and wages, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
Section~\ref{subsec:income-counterfactuals} uses these contrasts in illustrative
income scenarios for the 2025 workforce, holding hours and the estimated wage
structure fixed.

\subsection{Inference and Comparison Specifications}

Our main standard errors allow residual dependence across workers and years
within each sector-department cell. They do not allow unrestricted dependence
across departments within a sector. We therefore also report sector-clustered
inference, which permits that broader dependence but has only 17 clusters.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:preferred-clustering-sensitivity} holds the estimation
sample fixed and separates changes in the controls from changes in clustering.
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:preferred-extension-clustering} applies the same
sensitivity check to the endpoint changes and elasticities. These comparisons
keep the point estimates fixed when changing the covariance estimator.

The results therefore proceed from a common size premium with sequential
controls to the preferred formality-specific estimates. The appendix reports
alternative reference categories and additional comparisons, including a
size-by-formality model with sector-year effects but without occupational-position
or department controls, defined in Equation~(\ref{eq:formality-interaction}).
Those supplementary estimates do not replace the preferred within-formality
comparisons.

\subsection{Changes over Time}

We extend Equation~(\ref{eq:main}) by allowing the firm-size premiums, the
formality coefficient, and all worker and occupational-position coefficients
to vary by year:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preferred-dynamics}
  \ln(w_{isdt}) = \phi_t F_{isdt}
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0}(\beta_{gt}+\theta_{gt}F_{isdt})D_{ig}
  + X_{isdt}'\gamma_t+\rho_{o(i),t}
  + \lambda_{s\times d\times t}+\varepsilon_{isdt}.
\end{equation}
The fixed effects absorb the intercepts. Estimating this model separately by
year gives the annual profiles. Each regression includes the preferred worker
and occupational-position controls and sector-department fixed effects.
The annual samples partition the preferred pooled sample. This extension
allows changes in other conditional wage differences rather than forcing
them into the estimated size premiums.

To test the change between 2008 and 2025, we estimate both years jointly,
interacting all non-fixed-effect regressors with the 2025 indicator. The
point estimates reproduce the separate endpoint regressions. Joint estimation
also retains the covariance between the endpoint estimates within a
sector-department cluster. For informal workers, the null is
\(H_0:\beta_{g,2025}-\beta_{g,2008}=0\). For formal workers, it is
\(H_0:(\beta_{g,2025}+\theta_{g,2025})-(\beta_{g,2008}+\theta_{g,2008})=0\).
Tests are two-sided and pointwise. A positive difference means that the
conditional size-to-solo wage ratio increased. It does not establish a steady
annual rise or identify which group's wages changed.

\subsection{Approximate Firm-Size Elasticities}

GEIH reports firm size in categories. To compare our magnitudes with studies
reporting a continuous employer-size elasticity, we assign sizes of 1, 2.5,
4.5, 8, 15, 25, 40.5, 75.5, and 150 to the nine harmonized categories and estimate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preferred-elasticity}
  \ln(w_{isdt})=\alpha+\phi F_{isdt}
  +(\eta+\kappa F_{isdt})\ln(\widetilde{Size}_{isdt})
  +X_{isdt}'\gamma+\rho_{o(i)}+\lambda_{s\times d\times t}
  +\varepsilon_{isdt}.
\end{equation}
The coefficients \(\eta\) and \(\eta+\kappa\) are the informal and formal
elasticities, respectively. Both come from a single interacted regression,
with the preferred sample, controls, weights, and clustering. The coefficient
\(\kappa\) tests whether the slopes differ between formality groups. We also
report a raw log-log regression and a common-slope version imposing
\(\kappa=0\), with formality and the preferred controls included in the latter.

These slopes are approximate percentage wage differences associated with a
1 percent difference in assigned size, not elasticities based on observed
worker counts. They depend on the bin assignments, especially the value
assigned to 101+. They also impose a constant slope within each formality
group, whereas Equation~(\ref{eq:main}) allows each category to differ.
We compare them with the literature means reported by
\citet{DiegmannMullerSchoefer2026}, recognizing differences in populations and
controls. The appendix retains the simpler elasticity model in
Equation~(\ref{eq:elasticity}) for comparison. It also estimates a quadratic
extension with the preferred controls and formality interactions in
Equation~(\ref{eq:polynomial-size-profile}). That exercise tests whether two
parameters per formality group can summarize the categorical premiums and
checks sensitivity to the size assigned to 101+.
