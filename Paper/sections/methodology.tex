\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Preferred Specification}
\label{subsec:preferred-specification}

Our preferred specification allows the firm-size wage premium to differ between
formal and informal workers. It also accounts for occupational position and
compares workers within the same economic sector, department, and year:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
  \ln(w_{isdt}) = \alpha + \phi F_{isdt}
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0}(\beta_g+\theta_g F_{isdt})D_{ig}
  + X_{isdt}'\gamma + \rho_{o(i)} + \lambda_{s\times d\times t}
  + \varepsilon_{isdt}.
\end{equation}
Here, \(i\), \(s\), \(d\), and \(t\) index workers, sectors, departments, and
survey years, respectively. The outcome \(w_{isdt}\) is real hourly labor
income. The indicator \(F_{isdt}\) equals one for pension contributors and
zero for informal workers, and \(D_{ig}\) equals one if worker \(i\) reports
firm-size category \(g\). The reference
category \(g_0\) is solo work. The vector \(X\) contains a female-worker
indicator, age, age squared, and education dummies. Occupational-position fixed
effects \(\rho_{o(i)}\), where \(o(i)\) denotes worker \(i\)'s occupational
position, distinguish employment relationships, including
own-account work, employer status, and private- and public-sector wage
employment. Departments are Colombia's first-level territorial units, with
Bogot\'a treated as a department-equivalent Capital District.

The sector-department-year fixed effects absorb wage differences common to
workers in the same sector and department in a given year. The size
coefficients compare firm-size categories within these cells, separately by
formality and conditional on the included worker and occupational-position
controls. This is regression adjustment, not exact matching of workers on
every characteristic. Controlling for occupational position
is relevant because solo work and employment in larger firms differ substantially
in the prevalence of own-account and wage employment. These controls describe
conditional comparisons, rather than identifying the total effect of moving
from own-account work to a salaried job.

We estimate the model with GEIH expansion weights and cluster standard errors
by sector-department cells. Column (4) of
Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness} reports this
preferred specification. Columns (1)--(3) provide comparisons with progressively
more detailed geographic controls. The estimation sample for column (4)
contains 5,268,375 observations.

Within informal employment, the log-wage gap relative to solo work is
\(\beta_g\). Within formal employment, it is \(\beta_g+\theta_g\). For any
estimated log-wage contrast \(\widehat{\Delta}\), we report the percentage gap
as \(100[\exp(\widehat{\Delta})-1]\). These are ratios of exponentiated
conditional mean log incomes, not automatically ratios of arithmetic mean
incomes. Translating fitted log incomes into levels for the income scenarios
requires the additional retransformation assumption described in
Section~\ref{subsec:income-counterfactuals}. Statistical tests use the variance of the
full linear contrast, including the covariances between its coefficients.
For all regression specifications, two-sided tests and 95 percent confidence
intervals use a Student t distribution with \(G-1\) degrees of freedom, where
\(G\) is the number of clusters in the estimation sample. This gives 16 degrees
of freedom in the sector-clustered comparison models and 558 in the preferred
sector-department-clustered model. The same rule applies to coefficients and their linear
combinations, regardless of the reference category.

The model also distinguishes three comparisons. For an origin size \(g\) and a
destination size \(h\), which may be larger or smaller, they are:
\begin{align}
  \Delta^{F}_{g\to h} &= (\beta_h+\theta_h)-(\beta_g+\theta_g), \nonumber\\
  \Delta^{I}_{g\to h} &= \beta_h-\beta_g, \label{eq:preferred-contrasts}\\
  \Delta^{I\to F}_{g} &= \phi+\theta_g. \nonumber
\end{align}
The first two compare firm sizes within formal and informal employment,
respectively. The third compares formal with informal employment at the same
firm size, with informal workers as the denominator. We set
\(\beta_{g_0}=\theta_{g_0}=0\). Changing the omitted size category to 101+ or
11--19 workers leaves fitted log incomes unchanged. A given pairwise contrast
and its standard error are also unchanged, although the coefficients displayed
relative to the new base differ. These are adjusted wage comparisons. Interpreting them as the gains
from actual worker movements requires additional assumptions about selection
and the response of employers and wages, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
Section~\ref{subsec:income-counterfactuals} uses these contrasts in illustrative
income scenarios for the 2025 workforce, holding hours and the estimated wage
structure fixed.

\subsection{Comparison Specifications}

We retain a common-premium model to show how the overall size-wage association
changes as controls are added:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-premium}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0} b_g D_{ig}
  + X_{ist}'\gamma + \phi F_{ist}
  + \lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist}.
\end{equation}
Unlike Equation~(\ref{eq:main}), this model constrains the size coefficients to
be the same for formal and informal workers and omits occupational-position
controls. Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-wage-premium-regressions} starts with size
indicators alone, then adds sector-year fixed effects, worker characteristics,
and formality. This sequence is a descriptive benchmark, not the preferred
specification.

A second comparison retains size-by-formality interactions but uses the simpler
sector-year controls:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formality-interaction}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha + \widetilde{\phi}F_{ist}
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0}(\widetilde{\beta}_g+
       \widetilde{\theta}_g F_{ist})D_{ig}
  + X_{ist}'\gamma+\lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist}.
\end{equation}
The tildes distinguish these coefficients from the preferred model's estimates.
Tables~\ref{tab:formality-interaction-coefficients} and
\ref{tab:formal-wage-gap-by-size} report coefficients and formal-informal
gaps from this comparison model.
Both comparison models use GEIH weights and
sector-clustered standard errors. The preferred model additionally changes the
clustering level to sector-department, so differences in statistical significance
across the tables cannot be attributed only to the additional controls.
In particular, column (1) of
Table~\ref{tab:firm-size-local-geography-formality-robustness} and
Table~\ref{tab:formality-interaction-coefficients} report the same point
estimates. Their standard errors differ because the former clusters by
sector-department and the latter by sector.

\subsection{Dynamic Comparisons and Trend Test}

The dynamic exercises retain the sector-year benchmark controls. They describe
time variation in those conditional gaps and are not year-specific estimates
of Equation~(\ref{eq:main}). We first interact size categories with year:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-benchmark}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha
  + \sum_{\tau}\sum_{g\neq g_0} b_{g\tau}D_{ig}\mathbf{1}\{t=\tau\}
  + X_{ist}'\gamma+\phi F_{ist}
  + \lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist}.
\end{equation}
We also estimate a linear trend in each common size premium:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:trend-benchmark}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha
  + \sum_{g\neq g_0}(b_g+\delta_g Trend_t)D_{ig}
  + X_{ist}'\gamma+\phi F_{ist}
  + \lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist},
\end{equation}
where \(Trend_t=t-2008\). Positive values of \(\delta_g\) indicate a widening
gap relative to solo workers, and negative values indicate narrowing. We use
two-sided tests of \(H_0:\delta_g=0\).

For the formality-specific time profiles, we estimate the simpler interacted
model in Equation~(\ref{eq:formality-interaction}) separately by year, replacing
sector-year effects with sector effects. These exercises include sex, age, age
squared, and education, but not occupational-position or department controls.
They allow the size profiles to vary over time without imposing a linear trend.

\subsection{Approximate Firm-Size Elasticities}

The preferred model treats size as categorical, as reported by GEIH. To compare
with studies reporting continuous employer-size elasticities, we also estimate
an approximate log-log benchmark:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elasticity}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha+\eta\ln(\widetilde{Size}_{ist})
  + X_{ist}'\gamma+\phi F_{ist}
  + \lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist}.
\end{equation}
Assigned sizes are 1, 2.5, 4.5, 8, 15, 25, 40.5, 75.5, and 150 for the nine
harmonized categories from solo work through 101+. The elasticity depends on
these assignments, particularly for the open-ended 101+ category. We report a
raw estimate, the controlled full-sample estimate, and separate regressions for
formal and informal workers. The within-formality regressions omit the constant
formality indicator. The coefficient \(\eta\) is the approximate percentage
difference in hourly income associated with a 1 percent difference in assigned
firm size, holding the included controls fixed. The raw regression includes
only assigned log size. The controlled regressions include sector-year effects
and worker controls, but not occupational position or department. All use GEIH
weights and sector-clustered standard errors. We compare their magnitudes with
the literature summarized by \citet{DiegmannMullerSchoefer2026}, while recognizing
that bin assignments and control sets differ across studies.

We also fit a quadratic in assigned log size:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polynomial-size-profile}
  \ln(w_{ist}) = \alpha+\rho_1\ln(\widetilde{Size}_{ist})
  +\rho_2[\ln(\widetilde{Size}_{ist})]^2
  +X_{ist}'\gamma+\phi F_{ist}
  +\lambda_{s\times t}+\varepsilon_{ist}.
\end{equation}
This exercise asks whether a smooth curve summarizes the differences across
size bins under the same comparison controls as the elasticity exercise.
\citet{Terza1987} studies estimation with ordinal qualitative regressors, but
we do not implement that estimator. Our quadratic instead uses fixed numerical
values assigned to the GEIH intervals. Its implied elasticity is
\(\rho_1+2\rho_2\ln(\widetilde{Size})\), so the association may vary with size.
Neither the assigned sizes nor the polynomial replace the preferred
categorical specification.
