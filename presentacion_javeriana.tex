\section{Motivation}

\begin{frame}{Motivation}
  \idea{What can firm-size wage gaps tell us about the scope for higher labor incomes in Colombia?}
  \vspace{0.3cm}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Employment is concentrated in solo work and micro-firms.
    \item Larger firms tend to pay more: the firm-size wage premium.
    \item How much of the premium survives controls?
    \item Does the conditional premium differ between formal and informal workers?
    \item Has it increased or decreased between 2008 and 2025?
    \item What income gains does the premium imply under hypothetical worker reallocations?
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\section{Data and Definitions}

\begin{frame}{Data and Definitions}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item DANE's GEIH household survey, 2008--2025 excluding 2020.
    \item Workers aged 15 or older with positive income and valid hours in their main job.
    \item Outcome: real hourly labor income, using income and hours from the main job.
    \item The full sample includes salaried workers and non-salaried workers, principally own-account workers and employers. We repeat the analysis for salaried workers.
    \item Nine firm-size categories:  
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Solo, 2--3, 4--5, 6--10, 11--19, 20--30, 31--50, 51--100, and 101+.
    \end{itemize}
    %\item Solo work describes reported unit size, not necessarily own-account work.
    \item Formality defined by contribution to pensions.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
